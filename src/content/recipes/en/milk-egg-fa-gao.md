## Ingredients

- Plain or medium-protein flour, 200 g;
- Milk, 100 g for the softer version or 80 g for the springier version;
- Eggs, 2;
- White sugar, 30 g;
- Dried yeast, 2.5 g;
- Milk powder, 30 g;
- Butter, 10 g.


## Method

1. Crack the eggs into a large bowl. Add the milk and sugar and stir until even, dissolving as much of the sugar as possible.
2. Add the milk powder and stir until no obvious lumps remain.
3. Add the dried yeast and mix again.
4. Add the flour and stir thoroughly until no dry patches or large lumps remain, forming a smooth, fairly thick batter.
5. Cover the bowl and leave it somewhere warm for the first proof, until roughly doubled in volume.
6. Melt the butter and brush it evenly over the base and sides of a heatproof steaming bowl.
7. Stir the proved batter thoroughly with chopsticks to release large bubbles and make the structure even again.
8. Pour the batter into the buttered bowl and level the surface.
9. Set the bowl in the steamer without turning on the heat. Rest for 15 minutes for the second proof.
10. Steam for 30 minutes. Turn off the heat, wait 5 minutes, then uncover and remove the cake.

## Results

- **Softer version:** a fine, moist crumb with pronounced milk, egg, and butter aromas; softer and more open overall.
- **Springier version:** a similar flavor with more bounce and chew rather than an especially fluffy texture.

<div class="recipe-tips">

Tip:

Proofing time varies with the ambient temperature; judge it by the volume and condition of the batter. For an overnight first proof, I leave the covered batter outdoors until it reaches about 1.3 times its original volume, then refrigerate it overnight. By the next day it is usually close to doubled; check that it has proved sufficiently, then stir to release the gas and continue with filling the steaming bowl, the second proof, and steaming.

Butter makes the base and edges more aromatic; slight browning or natural cracks in places are normal.

To pack, briefly rest the cake after removing it from the steamer until the initial billow of steam has subsided, while the cake is still warm and giving off a little steam. Cut into pieces as needed, then wrap loosely in one layer of cling film, followed by a layer of aluminum foil or tin foil.
